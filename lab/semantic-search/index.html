01 · Try it

Keywords vs meaning

The same 160 short passages (16 topics, written for this lab) searched three ways: BM25 matches words, dense retrieval compares sentence embeddings from all-MiniLM-L6-v2, and hybrid fuses both rankings. Passage embeddings are precomputed; your query is embedded in the browser (a 23 MB model, downloaded once).

02 · Explore

The embedding space

Each dot is a passage, placed by the first two principal components of its 384-dimensional embedding and colored by topic. After a search, the diamond marks your query projected into the same plane, with lines to its five nearest passages. Topics form clusters without the model ever being told the topics.

03 · Measure

Recall@k on labelled queries

Ten queries with known relevant passages: six paraphrases that share few words with their answer, and four exact rare terms (a part number, “oxbow”, “roux”, place names). Recall@k is the share of relevant passages found in the top k; MRR averages 1/rank of the first relevant hit.

04 · Concepts

How it works

BM25

A probabilistic refinement of TF-IDF: score = Σ IDF(t) · tf·(k₁+1) / (tf + k₁·(1 − b + b·|d|/avgdl)) with k₁ = 1.5, b = 0.75. Term frequency saturates, long documents are normalized, and rare terms dominate. It only sees words the query and passage share.

Sentence embeddings

MiniLM is a small transformer distilled from a larger one and fine-tuned with a contrastive objective on a billion sentence pairs, so paraphrases land close together. Token vectors are mean-pooled and L2-normalized; similarity is the dot product (cosine).

Dense retrieval

Passages are embedded once, offline. At query time only the query is encoded, then compared with every passage. Real systems swap the linear scan for an approximate nearest-neighbor index (HNSW, IVF) to search millions of vectors.

Where each fails

Dense models can blur rare identifiers (part numbers, names) into generic meaning; BM25 can’t connect “pulse racing” with “tachycardia”. That complementary failure is why production search is usually hybrid.

Reciprocal rank fusion

Hybrid ranking here uses RRF: score(d) = Σsystems 1 / (60 + rank(d)). It needs no score calibration between systems and is a strong, simple baseline (Cormack et al., 2009).

The map is lossy

Two principal components keep only part of the variance of 384 dimensions, so the 2D distance between dots is a rough guide. Rankings are computed in the full space.
