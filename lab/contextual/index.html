03 · Concepts

How it works

Masked language modeling

BERT replaces 15% of input tokens (80% with [MASK], 10% random, 10% unchanged) and trains to recover them with a softmax over the vocabulary. Because the model sees both left and right context, it learns bidirectional representations rather than left-to-right ones.

Distillation

DistilBERT keeps 6 of BERT-base’s 12 layers and is trained to match the larger model’s output distribution, keeping most of its accuracy at about 60% of the size and much faster speed.

Static vs contextual

Word2Vec learns one vector per word type, a frequency-weighted blend of all its senses. Contextual encoders compute a vector per word token using self-attention over the sentence, so senses separate.

Reading the heatmap

Each cell is the cosine similarity of the target’s vectors in two sentences. Blocks of high similarity along the diagonal are senses. With Word2Vec, every cell would be exactly 1.00.

Anisotropy

Contextual vectors occupy a narrow cone, so even unrelated tokens have fairly high cosine similarity (Ethayarajh, 2019). Compare cells relative to each other rather than to zero.

Biases in predictions

Masked LMs reproduce associations from their training text: try “The nurse said that [MASK] would be late.” versus “The engineer said…”. Benchmarks such as CrowS-Pairs measure this systematically.
