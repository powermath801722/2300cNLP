Word Embedding Association Test

WEAT asks whether two sets of target words (X, Y) differ in how strongly they associate with two sets of attribute words (A, B), using cosine similarity in the embedding space. It mirrors the Implicit Association Test from psychology. Vectors come from the lab’s skip-gram Word2Vec model (text8, 30,000 words).

Separate words with commas or spaces. Lowercase only (text8 has no capitals). Editing any list switches the test to “Custom”.

02 · Try it

A gender direction and hard debiasing

Bolukbasi et al. (2016) estimate a single “gender direction” g from definitional pairs such as she–he, then neutralize words that should be gender-neutral by removing their component along g. Gonen & Goldberg (2019) showed the bias is still recoverable afterwards. Try both below.

Is the bias really gone? (Gonen & Goldberg test)

Take the 250 most female-leaning and 250 most male-leaning words among the 20,000 most frequent (by projection on g, excluding the definitional words), neutralize them, then cluster them into two groups with k-means. If clusters still line up with the original lean, gender information survives debiasing in the geometry between words, not just along g.

03 · Concepts

How it works

Association score

For a word w, s(w, A, B) = meana∈A cos(w, a) − meanb∈B cos(w, b). Positive means w sits closer to A than to B.

Test statistic & effect size

The lab uses S = meanx∈X s(x) − meany∈Y s(y). With equal set sizes this is Caliskan et al.’s sum statistic divided by |X|, so p-values match, and it stays fair when out-of-vocabulary words leave the sets unequal. Effect size d = S / stdw∈X∪Y s(w) (sample std), read like Cohen’s d: 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large.

Permutation p-value

Under the null hypothesis the X/Y labels are exchangeable. We reshuffle X∪Y into groups of the original sizes many times and report the one-sided p = (1 + #{Sperm ≥ S}) / (1 + N), so p can never be reported as exactly 0.

Gender direction

For each definitional pair, subtract the pair mean from both words; the first principal component of these centered vectors is g. Bolukbasi et al. found PC1 explains far more variance than the rest, suggesting gender is roughly one-dimensional in these spaces.

Hard debiasing (neutralize)

For gender-neutral words, w′ = (w − (w·g) g) / ‖w − (w·g) g‖. The projection on g becomes zero by construction, so a WEAT whose attributes are gendered words will usually shrink, but only along that one axis.

Why the clustering test matters

Words that leaned male still sit near other male-leaning words after neutralizing. Gonen & Goldberg call this “lipstick on a pig”: removing a direction hides bias from projection-based metrics without removing it from the representation.

Caveats

text8 is tiny (17M tokens) and the vocabulary is lowercase, so names collide with ordinary words (“bill”, “will”) and some test words are rare. Expect noisy, sometimes surprising results; treat them as a demonstration of the method, not a measurement of society.
