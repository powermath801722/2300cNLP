03 · Concepts

How it works

Why subwords

Word-level vocabularies can’t cover new words (out-of-vocabulary problem); character-level sequences are long and slow to model. Subwords sit in between: a fixed vocabulary that can still spell any word.

BPE training

Split words into characters plus an end-of-word marker </w>. Count adjacent symbol pairs weighted by word frequency, merge the most frequent pair everywhere, record the merge, repeat. The number of merges sets the vocabulary size.

BPE encoding

To segment a new word, start from characters and apply learned merges in the order they were learned (lowest rank first). The same procedure is deterministic, so training and inference agree.

Byte-level BPE (GPT-2)

Runs BPE over UTF-8 bytes, mapped to printable characters, so there is no unknown token: any string, emoji or script can be encoded. The cost is that rare characters split into several byte tokens.

WordPiece (BERT)

Chooses merges that most increase training-data likelihood rather than raw frequency, and at inference greedily matches the longest vocabulary prefix. Pieces after the first carry ##. Characters missing from the vocabulary become [UNK].

Unigram LM (T5 / SentencePiece)

Starts from a large candidate vocabulary and prunes pieces that least reduce corpus likelihood under a unigram model; segmentation is the most probable split (Viterbi). SentencePiece treats spaces as the symbol ▁, so it needs no language-specific pre-tokenizer.

Why it matters

Token counts set cost, context length and even model behavior: arithmetic suffers when numbers split oddly, and languages that need more tokens per word pay more and get less context. Tokenization is a modeling decision, not just preprocessing.
