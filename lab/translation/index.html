Translate, and watch the decoder choose

A MarianMT encoder–decoder from the OPUS-MT project translates English into French, German or Spanish. The decoder here is written out step by step on top of the model’s raw logits, so you can compare greedy and beam search and see the probability of every output piece. Each language direction downloads about 107 MB (quantized encoder + decoder) the first time.

02 · Concepts

How it works

Encoder–decoder

The encoder (6 transformer layers) reads the whole English sentence into contextual vectors. The decoder (6 layers) writes the target one piece at a time; at each step it attends to its own previous output (masked self-attention) and to the encoder vectors (cross-attention), then a softmax over the 59k-piece vocabulary gives the next-token distribution.

Greedy vs beam search

Greedy picks the single most likely piece at each step and can paint itself into a corner. Beam search keeps the k best partial translations, extends each with its most likely continuations, and keeps the best k again, approximating the most probable whole sentence. MarianMT systems are usually run with a beam of 4–6.

Length normalization

Every extra token multiplies in another probability below 1, so raw log-probability favours short outputs. Dividing by lengthα counteracts this: α = 0 is no normalization, α = 1 is per-token average. Set α to 0 and long inputs may get truncated translations from the beam.

Subwords

OPUS-MT uses SentencePiece: words are split into pieces, with ▁ marking the start of a word, so rare words, names and inflections can be built from known pieces instead of becoming unknown tokens.

BLEU

BLEU multiplies the geometric mean of 1- to 4-gram precisions (clipped by reference counts) by a brevity penalty for outputs shorter than the reference. Here a simple tokenizer splits punctuation and n ≥ 2 precisions are add-one smoothed so short sentences don’t score zero. For real evaluations use sacreBLEU, and report chrF or a learned metric such as COMET alongside it.

This model

Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-en-fr, -en-de and -en-es (and their reverse models for back-translation): transformer-base MarianMT models of about 75M parameters each, trained on the OPUS parallel corpora, converted to ONNX and quantized to 8 bits by Xenova.
