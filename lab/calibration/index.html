02 · Reliability

Reliability diagram, before and after temperature scaling

Predictions are grouped by confidence (the probability of the predicted class). A calibrated model’s bars sit on the diagonal. Temperature scaling divides the log-odds by a single number T fitted to minimize NLL on half of the data (the calibration split) and is evaluated on the other half (the test split). Accuracy can’t change; only confidence does.

03 · Selective prediction

Knowing when to abstain

Answer only the most confident fraction of inputs (the coverage) and measure the error rate on what you answer (the risk). A good confidence ranking makes risk fall quickly as coverage shrinks. Temperature scaling doesn’t reorder predictions, so it doesn’t change this curve at all.

05 · Concepts

How it works

Calibration

A classifier is calibrated if P(Y = ŷ | p̂ = p) = p for all p: among inputs where it reports confidence 0.8, 80% are correct. Accuracy says nothing about this; a model can be accurate and badly overconfident. Guo et al. (2017) showed that modern deep networks are often overconfident, and that much older, smaller networks were better calibrated.

ECE and MCE

Bin predictions by confidence into Bm; ECE = Σm (|Bm|/n) · |acc(Bm) − conf(Bm)| and MCE is the largest gap (Naeini et al. 2015). Both depend on the binning: equal-width bins leave high-confidence bins crowded and others nearly empty; equal-mass bins (Nixon et al. 2019) put the same number of examples in each. ECE is biased with few samples, so compare models with the same scheme.

Proper scoring rules

Negative log-likelihood −(1/n) Σ log p̂(yi) and the Brier score (1/n) Σ (p̂pos − yi)² are minimized in expectation only by the true probabilities, so they reward calibration and sharpness together. NLL punishes confident mistakes without bound; Brier caps each error at 1.

Temperature scaling

Rescale the logits, p̂ = σ(z / T), with one parameter fitted by minimizing NLL on held-out data (Guo et al. 2017). T > 1 softens overconfident models; T < 1 sharpens underconfident ones. It’s a special case of Platt (1999) scaling, preserves the ranking and hence accuracy, and assumes the test data look like the calibration data. Under distribution shift calibration degrades (Ovadia et al. 2019).

Domain shift, measured

DistilBERT was fine-tuned on SST-2 training data, the same distribution as this test set. Naive Bayes and logistic regression were trained on full-length movie reviews from NLTK (Pang & Lee 2004) and are tested here on short phrase-level sentences. Their lower accuracy and miscalibration show what moving between domains costs, even within “movie sentiment”.

Selective prediction

Sort by confidence κ(x), answer the top fraction, and plot risk against coverage. The area under that curve (AURC) summarizes the ranking; E-AURC subtracts the area an oracle would get from the same errors (Geifman et al. 2019). Choosing the largest coverage whose empirical risk is below a target is the basic selective classifier of Geifman & El-Yaniv (2017), which also gives finite-sample guarantees not computed here.
