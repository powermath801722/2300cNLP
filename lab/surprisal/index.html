How surprising is each word?

Surprisal is how unexpected a token is to a language model given everything before it: −log₂ P(token | prefix), measured in bits. It predicts human reading times, and it is the signal behind many detectors of machine-generated text.

02 · Concepts

How it works

Surprisal

The information content of an event is the negative log of its probability (Shannon, 1948). A token the model gave probability ½ carries 1 bit; one it gave 1/1024 carries 10 bits. Surprisal theory (Hale, 2001; Levy, 2008) proposes that the processing effort for a word is proportional to its surprisal in context.

s(wₜ) = −log₂ P(wₜ | w₁ … wₜ₋₁)

Perplexity

Average the surprisal over the N tokens and exponentiate: perplexity is the effective number of equally likely choices the model faced per token. Lower means the text was more predictable to this model. It is a property of a text–model pair, not of the text alone.

H̄ = (1/N) Σₜ s(wₜ) PPL = 2^H̄

Garden-path sentences

In “The horse raced past the barn fell” (Bever, 1970), readers take raced as the main verb, then hit fell and must reanalyze raced past the barn as a reduced relative clause. A language model shows the same spike: fell gets very high surprisal. Compare it with the unambiguous “The horse that was raced past the barn fell.” Surprisal predicts that the effect exists, though it often underestimates how large it is for humans (van Schijndel & Linzen, 2021).

GLTR: ranks, not just probabilities

GLTR (Gehrmann, Strobelt & Rush, 2019) colors each token by its rank in the model’s prediction: top-10 green, top-100 yellow, top-1000 red, beyond that purple. Text produced by likelihood-maximizing decoding is overwhelmingly green, while human writing has more yellow, red and purple: people pick words a model would not have bet on.

The first token and tokenization

GPT-2 needs some context to predict anything, so this page prepends the <|endoftext|> token as a start-of-text marker and scores every real token, including the first. Surprisal is per subword token: a rare word split into three pieces spends most of its surprisal on the first piece. To get word-level surprisal, sum over a word’s pieces. Leading spaces belong to the token (“ fell”), which is why tokens appear with their spacing.

Why detectors are unreliable

Low perplexity is evidence, not proof. Formulaic human writing (legal boilerplate, simple English, text by non-native writers) also scores low, and detectors built on perplexity have been shown to misclassify non-native writers’ essays as AI-generated (Liang et al., 2023). Sampling with higher temperature, paraphrasing, or a different scoring model shifts the statistics easily. DetectGPT (Mitchell et al., 2023) uses the curvature of log-probability under perturbations instead of raw perplexity, but no current method is reliable enough to accuse anyone.
