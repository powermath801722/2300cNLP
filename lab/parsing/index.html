03 · Concepts

How it works

Dependency trees

Every word attaches to exactly one head (the root attaches to an artificial ROOT), and each arc carries a grammatical relation such as nsubj, obj or obl. Universal Dependencies uses one label set across 100+ languages, and puts content words in charge (the noun heads a prepositional phrase, and the preposition is its case dependent).

Arc-standard transitions

A configuration is a stack, a buffer and the arcs built so far. SHIFT moves the next word onto the stack. LEFT-l makes the top word s0 the head of the one below it (s0 → s1) and pops s1. RIGHT-l adds s1 → s0 and pops s0. A sentence of n words always takes exactly 2n transitions, so parsing is linear time.

The classifier

At each step a linear model scores every legal action: score(a) = wa · φ(configuration), using about 40 feature templates (words and tags of the top three stack items and next three buffer words, their leftmost and rightmost dependents, distances and valencies). It's trained with the averaged perceptron, following a static oracle that knows the gold tree. The 2% of training sentences that are non-projective can't be produced by arc-standard, so they're skipped.

UAS and LAS

Unlabelled attachment score is the share of words given the right head; labelled attachment score also requires the right relation. Errors cascade: a greedy parser never revisits a decision, and wrong predicted tags cost several points (compare predicted-tag and gold-tag scores above). Beam search, dynamic oracles and neural features (Chen & Manning 2014; Kiperwasser & Goldberg 2016) close most of that gap. Current biaffine parsers reach about 90% LAS on EWT.

PCFGs

A probabilistic CFG gives each rule A → β a probability, with the rules for each left-hand side summing to 1. A tree's probability is the product of its rules, and the sentence's probability is the sum over all its trees. PCFGs make independence assumptions that real syntax violates, which is why lexicalized and latent-variable grammars were developed.

CKY

For a grammar in Chomsky normal form, cell [i, j] holds every nonterminal that can span words i+1…j. It's filled bottom-up: best[i,j,A] = max over k and A → B C of P(A → B C) · best[i,k,B] · best[k,j,C]. That takes O(n³ · |G|) time. Replacing max with sum gives the inside probability. The number of parses for a chain of prepositional phrases grows with the Catalan numbers, and the chart shares that structure instead of enumerating it.
