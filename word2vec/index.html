Words that keep the same company.

A Word2Vec model trained on 17 million words of Wikipedia, running entirely in your browser. Type a word to see its ten nearest neighbors by cosine similarity, then look at where they sit when 100 dimensions are flattened to two or three.

“You shall know a word by the company it keeps.” — J. R. Firth, 1957

    Separate up to 6 words with commas to compare their neighborhoods in one plot. The vocabulary is the 30,000 most frequent words, lowercase, and numbers are spelled out (seven, not 7).

    How it works

    Everything below was trained ahead of time with gensim; the browser only does lookups, dot products and PCA. The training script is in training/train_word2vec.py.

    Skip-gram

    For every word in the corpus, a shallow network learns to predict the words within a ±5 window around it. Negative sampling contrasts real context words with 10 random “noise” words. Words used in similar contexts end up with similar vectors.

    Nearest neighbors

    Vectors are normalized to unit length, so cosine similarity is just a dot product. The browser scores the query against all 30,000 vectors (8-bit quantized, ~3 MB) and keeps the top 10.

    2D & 3D views

    The plotted words are centered and projected onto their top principal components (PCA via power iteration). Axes are linear combinations of the 100 dimensions, so distances are approximate: trust the similarity scores over the picture.
